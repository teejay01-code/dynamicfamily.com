5 Ways to Communicate Without Fighting in Your Marriage

By Dr. Remy Nkeng | Professional Counsellor, Port Harcourt

Marriage is beautiful. But if we're honest, some weeks your home feels more like a debate stage than a safe place.

Silence. Shouting. Sarcasm.
For many couples in Port Harcourt and across Nigeria, those are the only 3 "communication styles" we learned.

But there's a 4th way: connection.

The Bible says, "Plans are established by counsel" - Proverbs 20:18. That means healthy homes are built on healthy communication.

Here are 5 practical ways you can start talking without fighting today:

1. Use "I Feel" Instead of "You Always"

When we feel hurt, our first instinct is to blame.
"You never listen to me!" shuts your spouse down.

Try this instead: "I feel unheard when I'm talking and the TV is on. Can we pause it for 10 minutes?"

"I feel" statements take ownership. They invite conversation instead of war.

2. Use The 20-Minute Rule

When voices rise and emotions get hot, your brain literally can't think straight.
Agree on a rule: When it's getting heated, we take 20 minutes to cool down, pray, then come back.

The goal isn't to win the argument. The goal is to understand each other.

"A soft answer turns away wrath" - Proverbs 15:1

3. Ask "Do You Want Advice or Just to Be Heard?"

Most men default to "fixing". Most women default to "sharing".
This causes frustration. He thinks she wants solutions. She thinks he doesn't care.

Before you give advice, ask: "Babe, do you want me to help you solve this, or do you just need me to listen right now?"

That one question can prevent 80% of fights.

4. Schedule a Weekly "State of The Union" Talk

Don't wait for problems to explode. Schedule 30 minutes every week. No phones. No kids.
Ask 3 questions:

  1. What went well for us this week?
  2. What hurt or bothered me?
  3. What do we need from each other next week?

Small, consistent check-ins prevent big blow-ups.

5. End Every Hard Talk With Prayer or Appreciation

Never go to bed with bitterness in your heart. Even after a tough conversation, find one thing to appreciate.
"Thank you for being honest with me, even though it was hard."

Prayer covers what words can't. Appreciation heals what criticism breaks.

Final Thought

Good communication is a skill, not a gift. It can be learned.

If you and your spouse keep having the same argument on repeat, it's not because you don't love each other. It's because you don't have the tools yet.

You don't have to figure this out alone.

How to Heal After Infidelity: 3 Steps Before You Decide to Stay or Leave

By Dr. Remy Nkeng | Professional Counsellor, Port Harcourt

Infidelity doesn't just break trust.
It breaks your world.

One day you think you know your marriage. The next day, everything feels like a lie.
The shock, the anger, the numbness, the questions — "Was any of it real?" — it's overwhelming.

If you're reading this, you're probably hurting deeply. And I want you to know: You're not crazy for feeling this way. You're not weak for being confused.

Healing is possible. Whether your marriage survives or not, you can heal.
Before you make any big decision, take these 3 steps:

Step 1: Stop The Bleeding

When someone is bleeding, you don't start physical therapy. You stop the bleeding first.

In infidelity, "stopping the bleeding" means:

  1. Full transparency - No more lies, no more hiding phones or deleting messages
  2. Cut all contact with the third party immediately. No "closure" conversations
  3. Create safety - Separate bedrooms if needed. Agree on boundaries for now

This isn't healing yet. This is triage. You can't think clearly or decide wisely while you're still in crisis mode.

Step 2: Process The Pain With Help

Don't do this alone. Please.

Pain shared with the wrong people turns into bitterness. Pain kept inside turns into depression.
You need a neutral, confidential space to process rage, grief, shame, and fear.

A professional counsellor helps you to:

  1. Regulate emotions so you don't make permanent decisions from temporary pain
  2. Understand what happened - without blaming yourself or excusing the betrayal
  3. Learn tools for communication, boundaries, and rebuilding or letting go

Trying to "pray it away" or "just forgive and move on" without processing often leads to the same wound reopening later.

Step 3: Decide From Healing, Not From Hurt

The biggest mistake couples make is deciding while they're still bleeding.

Ask these questions when things are a little calmer and you have support:

  1. Can trust be rebuilt? Are both people willing to do the hard, long work?
  2. Is there genuine remorse and repentance? Or just regret at being caught?
  3. What does forgiveness look like? Forgiveness doesn't mean forgetting. It means choosing not to use it as a weapon.
  4. What kind of marriage do we want to build now? You can't go back to the old one. You have to build a new one.

Some marriages don't survive infidelity. And that's okay. You can still heal and have a purposeful life.
But many marriages do survive — and they become deeper, more honest, and stronger than before.

Luke 4:18 "He came to heal the brokenhearted."

Final Thought

You don't have to figure this out alone at 2am.

Whether you're trying to rebuild or trying to find peace to walk away, you deserve support, clarity, and a safe place.
